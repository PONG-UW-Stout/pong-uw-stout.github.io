PONG has been throwing LAN parties at UW-Stout for about a quarter of a century. This is the long version.

Fair warning: the record is uneven. Some years we have signed waivers, budget spreadsheets and minute-by-minute tournament schedules. Other years we have a photo gallery folder with a date on it and nothing else. Where something is uncertain, it says so.

## Before there was a club

PONG started as a group of friends. In the late nineties they were playing in the basement of **JTC**, the building that holds Jeter, Tainter and Callahan halls, mostly in the Tainter coed lounge just outside the old cafeteria. No constitution, no budget, no officers. Just people hauling tower PCs across campus so they could sit in the same room and play together.


## 2001: it becomes official

PONG was founded in **2001**, formally recognised as a UW-Stout organization on **27 November** that year, approved during the 30th Congress of the Student Senate. Its first event followed in **2002**.

That founding date was rediscovered relatively recently, in Student Senate paperwork. For years the club believed it had started in 2003, which is why the twentieth anniversary LAN, Retro Reunion, was held in **February 2023**, by which point the real twentieth had already quietly gone past. The club now dates itself from 2001 and has settled the question for good.

The name is a backronym: **People's Organization of Network Gaming**. Whether the Atari game came first is not written down anywhere, though it is hard to imagine it did not.

The founding president is **Eric Landru**, who ran the club through its first years and later came back as an advisor after graduating and taking a job on campus. 

By 2002 the board had five seats: president, vice president, treasurer, secretary, and public relations. That is the whole club, on paper, in one line.

The earliest surviving trace of an actual event is a photo gallery folder on the old website dated **26 October 2004**. Everything between that first event in 2002 and that folder is gone.

## The small hall years, 2004 to 2008

This era is documented almost entirely by one unglamorous thing: **signed waivers**.

Every non-Stout attendee had to sign a risk awareness form, and somebody scanned the stacks and kept them. One page per person, which makes them an accidental headcount.

| Event | Date | Signed waivers |
|---|---|---|
| PONG LAN | 30 January 2005 | about 34 |
| PONG LAN | 31 March 2006 | about 79 |
| Spring Fever LAN | 9 February 2007 | about 20 |
| Thanksgiving Charity LAN | 16 November 2007 | about 88 |
| PONG LAN | 8 February 2008 | about 57 |

Those are undercounts. Stout students checked in with an ID swipe and never signed anything, so the waivers only capture guests and visitors. The real rooms were bigger.

The photo galleries from the same years fill in the names: Advisement LANs, Spring Fever, April Fools LAN, StoutNic, Founders Day, Backyard Bash, and the Thanksgiving LAN. More on all of that in [The Names](lan-names).

The club was also already chasing sponsors. The earliest sponsorship pitch on record is from **March 2005**, for the April Fool's LAN, claiming "anticipated attendees are over 100". ATI, Nvidia, Corsair, Cooler Master and Bawls all came aboard. Seagate was marked, in the tracker, "NO GOOD".

The oldest surviving piece of PONG artwork is the logo itself, drawn on **8 April 2006**.

## The exile years

In the early 2010s the Memorial Student Center went in for renovation, and PONG lost its room.

The club moved out of Huff's Lounge and into the game design room in Micheels Hall, the one with the green screen, down the hallway that connects to the Jarvis science wing. Posters from 2011 and 2012 advertise twelve-hour, daytime-only LANs in **184 Micheels Hall**, which is a lecture room.

A long way down from a ballroom. The club called the stretch the **exile years**, and the name stuck partly because it rhymed with the president in exile.

Then the MSC reopened, and PONG moved into the Great Hall properly. There are photos of **Cody Oldenburg**, the designer at the time, setting up tables at a 2010 LAN in the recovered space. Apart from COVID, PONG has been in the MSC ever since.

The move changed what a LAN could be. In **October 2012** OctÜberfest ran thirty hours in the Great Hall; the year before it had been twelve hours in a lecture room. More on all of this in [Where PONG Has Played](venues).

## The build-out, 2011 to 2014


The **constitution** gets rewritten twice. The 2009 version had eight officers. A 2011 revision added a Secretary, because the Student Senate required one. The **Fall 2014 constitution** is the big one: eleven officers, an explicit rule that the vice president automatically inherits the presidency, a two-year commitment required of VP candidates, a non-discrimination clause, and an emergency replacement procedure requiring a unanimous exec vote.

The **network** gets serious. A Cisco Catalyst core switch named CoreCat arrives in 2013. The year after, the whole thing is redesigned around three stacked switches called SUPERCORE, SERVERCORE and SUPERBALLS. The access switches are all named after cat memes, a tradition that traces back to a 2010 3Com unit with "CEILING CAT" written into its firmware. See The Network.

The **website** gets ambitious. In February 2012 the club launches **ALP**, its own LAN party portal, written in-house. You could register for the next LAN, pick your seat off a floor plan, browse the tournament schedule and sign up for brackets. It also, for reasons nobody wrote down, tracked your caffeine intake. See [The Website and the Forums](the-website-and-the-forums).

The **money** gets formal, and occasionally absurd. The club runs a cable sale every move-in weekend, selling hand-made Ethernet and coax patch cables out of a tent outside Price Commons to fund LAN prizes. In 2012 it asks the Student Senate for $4,420 to run a "Stout Technology Expo", including $1,500 for a police officer. The Senate approves exactly zero dollars of it. See Sponsors and Money.

And the LAN names get completely out of hand, in the best way. Forever A LAN. March Fools LAN. Winter WonderLAN. OctÜberfest. Ninja LAN. Space LAN. Classy LAN.

## Peak documentation, 2014 to 2016

If you want to know what a PONG LAN was actually like, this is the window where the archive can tell you minute by minute.

**Forever A LAN, February 2014** is the best-documented event in club history, because the ALP database survived. Five hundred registered attendees. Fifteen tournaments. Twenty-four teams, one hundred and one recorded matches. A seating chart with 784 seats mapped to pixel coordinates on a floor plan.

The tournament list from that weekend is a decent summary of the era's sense of humour. League of Legends and DOTA 2 and CS:GO and Smash, yes. But also Minecraft Spleef, Meme Jeopardy, two-person Surgeon Simulator where one player has the keyboard and the other has the mouse, a thing called the **$1 Chug** whose rules note carefully explains that it does not cost a dollar, and the **Download Tournament**, whose entire rules text reads: *"Delete your whole steam library and re-download it! Whoever has the most terabytes wins!"*

Divekick had a rule that the winner "must run around the entire lan yelling 'divekick divekick I won at divekick!'"

**Expo LAN, October 2014** produced the only hard attendance number anywhere in the club's records. A draft blog post for the admissions office recorded **220 students, friends and alumni** in the Great Hall across the weekend.

That same year, PONG hosted a **League of Legends All-Stars viewing party** in the Cedar/Maple room. Riot Games noticed, recognised PONG as an official collegiate club, and started sending free merchandise.

**LAN Before Time, March 2015** was a dinosaur pun that paid off literally: a box of Quaker Dinosaur Eggs oatmeal handed out as a prize. The weekend also produced the single best complaint in the minutes, when daylight saving time ate an hour of a 48-hour event: *"LAN is actually 47 hours...(DST pls)"*

**WasteLAN, November 2015** pulled the biggest sponsor haul on record, including five in-game CS:GO knives worth about a hundred dollars each as first prize.

By **September 2015**, Expo LAN had outgrown a single room and spread across the Great Hall *and* the Crystal Ballrooms, with reserved seating split between them and a separate walk-in category.

The rules got serious the same year. The 2015 rewrite banned vaping, banned propping the doors open, capped monitors at 36 inches, guaranteed every attendee three feet of table, and produced a banned-items list that doubles as a list of things that had already gone wrong: no UPS units, no coffee makers, no mini fridges, no fans, no power strips.

## The quiet stretch

The club's shared drive effectively stops in **March 2017**. The last sponsorship tracker for that year carries the previous year's contacts forward almost unchanged and records not one new confirmed sponsor. The forums had already been retired in March 2016 in favour of a Facebook group and a brand new Discord server. The Minecraft server had gone quiet in August 2014 and was never revived.

None of this means the club stopped. LANs kept running. It means the people running them stopped filing paperwork into a shared Dropbox, because the conversation had moved to Discord and nobody was archiving that.

So for roughly nine years, what we have is names and memories rather than documents.

What we do know: the roles kept evolving. **Server Administrator** split off from Network Administrator in 2018, after Will Dean, in the words of the club's own notes, "forced Server Administrator to exist". **Designer** separated back out from Promoter around 2020, having been folded into it since 2012.

## COVID


The club survived, and it did not go dark. **Every event during COVID moved into the PONG Discord server and ran virtually**

The current board is fairly direct that this was not a given, and credits the management work Aestris did that year with keeping the club alive. She came back as an advisor in 2022 and still holds the seat.

## The modern era

The LAN names keep coming, and the schedule has settled into a rhythm. Four LANs per academic year, roughly September, November, February and April, each one 48 hours in the MSC Great Hall and Ballrooms, free to get in.

It was not always four. In earlier years PONG ran as many as six in a year, each of them shorter.

ApocaLAN. PirateLAN. SuperLAN. Wild West LAN. FantasyLAN. BattleLAN. Retro Reunion, the twentieth anniversary. LANime. Expo-LAN. Galacta-LAN. Tropical-LAN. Build-A-LAN, where the schedule was built by the people who turned up. Retro-LAN. Cozy-LAN, with the string lights up. Classy-LAN, on Valentine's weekend. Safari-LAN. Mil-LAN-ium. Spooky-LAN. Candy LAN. Festi-LAN. LAN-tasy. The full list with dates is on the [LANs page](/lans/).

Some other things that happened along the way:

**The mentor system.** Starting with Ashe in 2021, past execs get invited back a year after their term to advise the current board. It solves the problem every student organization has, which is losing its entire institutional memory every four years.

**LAN Staff.** Formalised in 2024, capped at six people per board, no term limits. There had always been volunteers who helped with setup and teardown, and this gave them a title


**Mabel Juice.** Somewhere around 2023 a Gravity Falls joke turned into a tradition. See [Mabel Juice](stories/mabel-juice).

## Where things stand

As of the 2026-2027 school year, PONG runs on  a board of eleven, a handful of LAN staff, two mentors and two advisor who used to be president.

The season is four LANs: **LAN-tasy**, **LAN-Prix**, **LAN-iversary** and **At-LAN-tis**.

PONG is still free to attend, still fills the Great Hall, and still runs a tournament where somebody chugs a dollar soda for no prize. Not bad for a group of friends who started out in a basement lounge.
