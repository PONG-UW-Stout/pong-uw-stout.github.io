PONG is a student organization, which means that underneath the LAN parties there is a constitution, an election cycle, and an annual argument with a budget committee. The paperwork survives better than almost anything else, so we know a lot about this.

## The constitutions

**2009.** The earliest constitution we have, authored on **27 September 2009**. Eight positions: President, Vice President, Treasurer, Promoter, Web Master, Event Coordinator, Sponsorship, and Advisor. Meetings at least every two weeks. And a clause that has survived every rewrite since:

> PONG shall not have a membership fee unless ratified and approved by the executive board and voted into existence by at least 2/3 members at pre-announced bi-weekly meeting.

**2011.** A revision adds **Secretary** as a ninth position, because the Student Senate required organizations to have one.

**Fall 2014.** The big rewrite, finalised on 30 September 2014. Eleven positions, and three new ideas that still shape the club:

- **The vice president inherits the presidency.** Written in exactly those terms: *"This position is inherited through the Vice President position once the current President leaves his/her position."* VP candidates had to commit to two years, because running for VP meant signing up to be president.
- **A non-discrimination clause**, covering race, gender, religion, ancestry, age, veteran status, marital status, sexual orientation, income, physical ability and political ideology.
- **An emergency replacement procedure.** If an officer cannot continue, the board draws up a shortlist, discusses it, and must reach a **unanimous** vote before approaching anyone. If that person declines, start again.

The purpose statement from the 2014 document is worth quoting, because it is a surprisingly good description of what the club actually does:

> To involve students of the University of Wisconsin Stout and the community with a valuable recreation experience, and to aid in the development of skills related to the organization and their majors.

The club's own amendment memo from around the same time is less lofty, and includes a proposal to require a "method of order" at meetings, on the grounds that *"Meetings tend to get very out of control and take far longer than they need to."*

## Elections

Elections run at the second general meeting of the spring semester, in March. Nominees have to be present and recognised members. One nomination per person per position. Candidates give a two to three minute presentation and take questions. If you run unopposed, you win by default.

Voting is one secret ballot per member. The president and the advisor do not vote; they count the ballots.

A two-thirds exec vote can impeach an officer or amend the constitution. The constitution has to be reviewed every two years, and immediately after any "drastic change", defined as two thirds of the board turning over, or half plus the president or vice president.

Everything about this works except the part where people have to run. The spring 2014 election produced a docket where the Treasurer line reads, simply, **"N/a"**. Nobody ran. The seat was filled mid-year instead, with a meeting note in September that just says *"Welcome our new Treasurer Psarahdactyl."*

Campaign presentations were a real format, and at least one of them was a complete shitpost. The 2014 Tournament Coordinator deck included a slide of fake testimonials attributed to Batman, Jesus Christ, Emma Watson, Gandhi, and the candidate's own father, whose quote reads: *"I hope you're not seriously going to use this power point."* He won the seat.

## The roles, and how they moved

PONG's org chart has been rearranged more than once.

**Sponsorship Liaison** became **Ambassador**. Same seat, new name, somewhere around the early 2020s.

**Tournament Coordinator** became **Games Coordinator**. 

**Designer** was folded into **Promoter** between roughly 2012 and 2019, then split back out. For most of the 2010s, the person making your posters was the same person running the Facebook page.

**Promoter and Ambassador** formally split in 2022-2023

**Network Administrator** split into **Network Administrator** and **Server Administrator** around 2018. Before the split, one person ran the LAN network and the year-round game servers, which had quietly become two jobs.

**Secretary** existed from 2011 to 2020. It was the note-taker and, later, ran the Twitch stream. The duties eventually moved to Ambassador and the seat was retired.

**Mentor** started in 2021 with Ashe Gunderson. Recent Past execs get invited back to advise the current board. It exists because a student org forgets everything it knows every four years.

**LAN Staff** was formalised in 2024 capped at six per board, with no fixed term. Volunteers had always helped with setup and teardown; this gave them a title. A few people have been declared LAN Staff retroactively for work they did years earlier.


## The yearly rhythm

Under all of it there is a calendar that has barely changed:

- **Cable sale**, the Sunday and Monday of the first week, from a tent outside Price Commons. Profits buy LAN prizes.
- **Backyard Bash**, usually the first Tuesday, for recruitment.
- **First general meeting**, the day after.
- **Org Renewal**, due around 30 September.
- **Attend to Spend**, the budget hearing.
- **Four LANs**, roughly September, November, February and April.
- **Elections** in March.
- **Leadership Summit**, which one or two execs attend, described in the binder as a *"Generic organization motivation event"*.
