title: The Planetside 2 Account Purge
description: Two hundred people joined the same game from the same network at the same time, and every single one got banned.

PONG ran Planetside 2 as a LAN event. Planetside 2 is a massively multiplayer shooter where hundreds of players fight over a planet, which makes it close to ideal for a room with two hundred people in it.

During the game's launch weekend, PONG got between 150 and 200 people at the LAN to sign up and jump in, all at once.

## What the game saw

From the game's perspective, this is what happened: in the space of a few minutes, roughly two hundred brand new accounts connected from a single network, all from the same block of university IP addresses, all behaving identically.

The anti-cheat system drew the obvious conclusion and banned every single one of them.

Everyone at the LAN who tried to play Planetside 2 that weekend lost their account.

## The fix

For roughly the next three years, PONG had to email Sony Online Entertainment, later Daybreak, **ahead of every LAN**, with the club's public IP address and the dates of the event, so that the game's fraud systems would know that the incoming wave of connections was a student organization and not a bot farm.

In the words of **Buddha**, who was network admin at the time and was there:

> for the following like 3 years we had to email SOE/Daybreak ahead of the LANs with our public IP and the dates of the event so they knew we weren't a bot farm lmao

## When this happened

The weekend of **24 November 2012**, which was the game's launch weekend.

The club's 2026 history recording places it "around 2015", which is three years out. The 2012 date is the right one.

## The afterlife

Planetside 2 stuck around as a PONG institution well beyond the incident. A draft blog post written for the admissions office in 2014 describes it as one of the club's defining games:

> PONG directs their own platoon that performs raids during LANs and whenever they can get together, either physically or virtually.

"Planetside 2 Raid" appears as a scheduled slot on tournament sheets through 2014 and 2015, usually run by the network admin.

So the club got every account at a LAN permanently banned by a game, and then kept playing that game as a flagship activity for years. Which is, on reflection, extremely PONG.
