**9 October 2013.**

PONG had built its own LAN party portal, [ALP](../the-website-and-the-forums), and it was good. Registration, seating charts, tournament brackets, prize voting. Then, while staff were setting it up for the next LAN, it ate four hundred people.

## What happened

The announcement posted to ALP's own news system is the cleanest account:

> So, in the process of trying to set up the ALP system for our next LAN ... ALP went and dropped the first 400 users from the User database.

Those four hundred accounts were the club's registration history: everybody who had signed up through ALP since it launched in February 2012. The database never recovered its original numbering, and user IDs effectively restart from that point, which is why the surviving snapshots look the way they do.

## The thread

Somebody started a thread about it on the forums, titled:

> Alp accidentially the entire user database

It pulled **20,361 views**. Only two threads ever beat it: "EPIC Gifs and Lulz!" at 39,755 and the Child's Play Charity LAN thread at 36,966. An outage announcement is the third most-read thing the club ever posted.

The title is the part that lasted. "Accidentially the entire user database" is the meme construction of its era, deployed at exactly the right moment by somebody who was presumably quite annoyed.

## The aftermath

ALP kept running for years afterwards. By the last surviving database snapshot in May 2016 it held **1,781 registered users**, more than three times the count at the time of the wipe, and was already taking pre-registrations for that September's Expo LAN. It recovered fine. The reason the club's registration records from 2012 and early 2013 are so thin is this.
