A LAN party is mostly a question of whether you can get enough power and enough tables into one room. PONG's history is, to an unreasonable degree, the history of which room.

## The basement, late 1990s to early 2000s

Before PONG was an organization it was a group of friends in the basement of **JTC**, the building that holds Jeter, Tainter and Callahan halls, mostly using the Tainter coed lounge just outside the old cafeteria.

A diagram of this era survives, shared years later by past president DaVermenator. It is a layout of the LANs held in the JTC basement, and the arrangement was driven almost entirely by where the outlets were.

## Huffs Lounge

At some point the club moved up into **Huffs Lounge**, in the Memorial Student Center. Exactly when is not recorded.

## The Ballrooms, 2009 to 2011

By 2009 PONG was running events in the MSC Crystal Ballrooms, and the evidence is a UW-Stout facilities work order dated **16 October 2009**, filed under the account name "Pong", mapping every electrical circuit in Crystal Ballrooms A, B and C.

That document gives the hard ceiling the club spent the next decade designing around:

| Room | Outlets | Amps |
|---|---|---|
| Ballroom A | 13 | 69 |
| Ballroom B | 12 | 116 |
| Ballroom C | 13 | 53 |
| **Total** | **38** | **240** |

Thirty-eight outlets. That single number explains basically every power rule PONG ever wrote: the two-outlet limit per person, the ban on power strips, the ban on mini fridges and coffee makers and space heaters, and the long-running insistence that you may not plug a power strip into another power strip.

The club also kept a Great Hall electrical circuit map dated **7 February 2006** and was still using it years later, because a decade-old facilities diagram is still a facilities diagram.

## The exile years

Then the MSC went in for renovation and PONG lost the room.

The club moved to the game design room in Micheels Hall, the one with the green screen, in the hallway connecting to the Jarvis science wing. Posters from this stretch advertise LANs in **184 Micheels Hall**, running 10am to 10pm. Twelve hours, daylight only, in a lecture room.

Three of them survive: OctÜberFest on 8 October 2011, an April LAN on 30 April 2011, and a December LAN with two different colour variants of the same poster design, suggesting somebody could not decide.

This is the stretch the club calls the **exile years**, which doubles as a joke about Dan Wald, the president in exile, who had vanished from campus a few years earlier.

## The Great Hall

The MSC reopened and PONG moved into the Great Hall properly, around 2010. There are photos of designer Muffin setting up tables in the recovered space.

The difference is immediate and measurable. In October 2011 OctÜberfest was twelve hours in a lecture room. In **October 2012** OctÜberfest was **thirty hours in the Great Hall**. By early 2013 LANs were running forty-eight hours.

Two things made that possible. The first is that the Great Hall has the power, so the club stopped having to think about amperage and started relying on the campus Event Technology crew, with whom PONG has a long working relationship. The second is that the Great Hall is big enough that the event could become a place rather than a room booking.

By **September 2015**, Expo LAN had outgrown even that and spread across the Great Hall *and* the Crystal Ballrooms at once, with reserved seating split between the two and a separate walk-in category for people who turned up without a seat.


## Today

Four LANs a year, each one 48 hours, in the MSC Great Hall and Ballrooms. Free to get in.

The only time since the renovation that PONG has not been in the MSC was during COVID, when every event moved into the club's Discord server and ran virtually.
