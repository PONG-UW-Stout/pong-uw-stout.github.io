Some of these are twelve years old and some are three. Between them they are most of what separates a PONG LAN from a room with Wi-Fi in it.

## The $1 Chug

The longest-running named tradition the club can document, appearing on every tournament schedule from late 2014 onwards, across every board, venue and theme.

A rules note survives in the old tournament database, clearly written by somebody tired of being asked:

> Please note, this is a FREE tournament. $1 Chug is just its name. It does NOT cost $1 to participate!

The name comes from the soda, which costs about a dollar at the shop, and the event is what it sounds like: you chug it. It is still run today.

The current board reckons this kind of thing, cheerful and low-stakes rather than seriously competitive, sits closer to what PONG actually is than the brackets do.

## Mabel Juice

A Gravity Falls joke that became a fixture. It has its own article: [Mabel Juice](stories/mabel-juice).

## Meme Jeopardy

On the schedule since at least February 2014, sitting alongside the real tournaments. It still runs, now called PONG Jeopardy. Same event, new name, with **2024** the earliest point anyone can place the change.

It takes real work to assemble, which is why the standing rule is that it has to be finished a month before the LAN and needs at least two people on it.


## The prize ticket economy

Rather than cash prizes, PONG ran on tickets. The system as of late 2015:

- 2 tickets for first place
- 1 ticket for second
- 1 ticket for third
- 1 extra ticket for helping set up the LAN

Tickets went into prize drawings, and every multi-day schedule budgeted exactly two of those, one mid-event and one near the close. Handing out a ticket for helping with setup is a neat bit of club management, and it is the reason the tables ever went up on time.

## The cable sale

Every move-in weekend, PONG puts up a tent outside Price Commons and sells hand-made Ethernet and coax patch cables to students wiring up their dorm rooms. Setup around 7am, teardown around 5pm. Profits fund LAN prizes.

The cable sale still runs every year.
